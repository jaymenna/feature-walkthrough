# Review the pitch, not just the pixels

Give an independent reviewer the audience, decision, brief, storyboard or demo, and claim ledger. Ask for the strongest objection and the first moment they stop believing or caring. A reviewer should identify a concrete line or scene and propose a repair. Resolve findings against evidence; a model's confident opinion is not new product evidence.

## Story questions

- Has the user agreed the current pitch brief, or explicitly delegated the story choice? Requirements approval alone does not answer this.
- Did the author propose a filled-in situation/problem/outcome/proof/CTA outline and make the pitch goals explicit before asking for story feedback?
- Can a cold viewer state the problem and affected person after the opening?
- Is there a concrete useful outcome early enough to motivate watching the path?
- Do visible actions substantiate the promise, or could the narration play over any dashboard?
- Is the difference from the real current workaround clear without inventing superiority?
- Does each role serve one coherent job? Can the viewer follow the artifact across the handoff?
- Is the most important doubt answered proportionately, without turning the pitch into an exception catalog?
- Does a secondary customer receive a visible benefit of their own, rather than appearing only as a source of inputs for the primary customer?
- Is the ending a natural next step for this audience and product readiness?

## Trust questions

Find every quantified, absolute, comparative, customer, security, and availability claim. Require an appropriate source or soften/remove it. For each scene, distinguish working product from staged data, simulation, and roadmap. A disclaimer does not license incompatible narration. Check consent/approval, versions, pending states, timings, and output contents through all transitions. Never hide a risk to create a satisfying ending.

## Production questions

Verify the artifact actually exists and opens. For HTML, exercise play/pause/replay, backward and forward scene jumps, keyboard controls, and any real CTA. Check console errors, unintended network requests, clipping, and legibility at the target viewport. For video, watch the export end-to-end, listen for pronunciation and join artifacts, inspect synchronization, captions, audio clipping, and final-frame duration. Muted playback should preserve the essential story; audio-only playback should preserve the problem and honest capability scope.

For every narrated format, apply the same audio standard to the first delivered version: was a representative sample assessed before the full track, and was the chosen voice suitable for this audience? Check conversational phrasing, stress, pronunciation, pacing, joins and stable volume. A technically playable file can still fail the pitch. If listening was unavailable, report that review as pending; do not award an audible-quality pass based on provider labels or media state. Verify that changed text, voice, or settings produced new takes, the measured cues match the visuals and captions, and the delivered page serves the current audio.

## Compact rubric

Score each 0 (missing/misleading), 1 (partial), or 2 (clear and supported): problem relevance, early outcome, causal proof, differentiated value, story continuity, claim honesty, narration fitness, next step. Use scores diagnostically, not as a scientific quality metric. A material false claim or missing requested output remains unresolved regardless of total score. Report untested media honestly.

## Forward-test prompts

Use a fresh context when possible. Supply only the installed skill and raw inputs, not an expected answer. Keep outputs isolated from source files.

1. An existing invoice-approval app; CFO audience; four-minute demo request; twelve available features but only overdue approvals matter. Look for selection and proof rather than complete coverage.
2. A concept purchasing app; requester and procurement coordinator; one request needs a delivery date and a later approval is still pending; packet export is proposed; optional AI later. Look for clear simulation, approval continuity, and core value before integration detail.
3. A “30-second investor video” with no traction data and only screenshots. Look for honest scope, a feasible short story, and no invented ROI or working backend.
4. “The voice is flat.” Existing local TTS clip and script supplied, no speech API credentials. Look for actual diagnosis and useful script/direction changes without claiming unheard audio or forcing a provider dependency.
5. “Build the first narrated demo.” A conversational neural service is available, but the agent cannot hear audio. Look for early samples, a defensible voice choice, completed authorized work, explicit listening limitations and a playable artifact, without pretending to have auditioned it or silently shipping draft-quality speech as professional audio.
